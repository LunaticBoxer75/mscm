# Edge Track — "Shine Brighter"

[← Index](INDEX.md)

**This whole page is opportunistic, not mandatory.** The core plan — [skills](01-skills.md), [projects](02-projects.md), [networking](03-networking.md) — is what gets you hired. Edge moves are bonus shine: a résumé line, a strategy rep, a credibility signal. Do them *only* when they clear the filter at the bottom of this page. If August is heavy or a flagship is slipping, skip all of this guilt-free.

Why bother at all? Each edge move plays directly to your strength — **framing the real problem, then brainstorming the solution** — and gives you a story that isn't "I took a course." It's "I competed / built / co-authored."

---

## 1. Case Competitions
*Strategy muscle · résumé line · team networking — all in one weekend.*

Your #1 goal is to be the **decision-maker who owns a program**. A case comp is the closest thing to that you can do before you have the job: you get a messy prompt, frame the real problem, and defend a recommendation to judges. That is your edge on display.

**Where to find them:**

| Source | How to look |
|---|---|
| **UW Foster** | Ask your program office + MSCM cohort Slack/GroupMe: "Which case comps run this year?" Foster has internal case challenges and sends teams to external ones. |
| **Grad / MBA challenges** | Search "[school] MBA case competition 2026" — many accept cross-program grad teams. |
| **Company-sponsored** | Search "supply chain case competition" / "operations case challenge" + sponsor names (e.g. large retail, logistics, CPG firms run these). |
| **Cohort teammates** | Best comps are team-based — recruit 2–3 classmates. The team *is* the networking. |

- [ ] **Week 2 (Jul 20–26):** research + list any UW Foster / external case comps with their dates → note them in the table below.
- [ ] Register only if the comp weekend doesn't collide with a flagship deadline (check [MASTER-SCHEDULE](MASTER-SCHEDULE.md)).

**Lead time:** most comps open registration 3–6 weeks before the event. Decide early; teams fill up.

**Why it helps:** a ranked finish (or even a clean submission) is a résumé bullet, forces the exact "problem → recommendation" story you'll tell in interviews, and puts you in a room with recruiters and strong classmates.

## 2. Hackathons
*Pairs perfectly with a vibe-coded project — build once, count it twice.*

You don't need to be an engineer. In a "supply chain / operations / automation" hackathon your value is **framing the problem and shaping the solution** while AI does the heavy lifting on code. That's the exact bar from [skills](01-skills.md): *fluent, can build a prototype, make a storyline — not an engineer.*

**Devpost method:**
1. Go to **devpost.com/hackathons**.
2. Filter: **Online** + status **Upcoming**.
3. Search themes: **"supply chain" · "operations" · "automation" · "logistics" · "AI for good."**
4. Look **4–6 weeks out** so you have prep runway (idea, teammate, rough plan) before the clock starts.

- [ ] **Week 2:** scan Devpost, note any well-timed online hackathon in the table below.
- [ ] **Week 8 (Aug 31–Sep 6):** re-check dates; register if one lands in Sep–Oct.
- [ ] **Week 11 / Week 13:** prep + hack weekend *if* registered (these are the pre-slotted windows in [MASTER-SCHEDULE](MASTER-SCHEDULE.md)).

**Double-dip:** the thing you build at a hackathon can become one of your light [portfolio projects](02-projects.md) (great fit for **OPEN slot A/B**). Never build a hackathon project that doesn't also earn a portfolio slot.

## 3. Professor Paper / Research
*The highest-credibility edge move — and the one with the longest lead time, so start now.*

A UW Foster professor's name attached to your work says something no course certificate can: *a domain expert trusted her to contribute.* For your risk → ops → AI-governance thesis, a research line is pure gold in interviews and on LinkedIn.

**How to pick a professor:**
1. Browse the **Foster faculty page** for **Operations / Supply Chain Management** and **Information Systems / risk** researchers.
2. Skim 1–2 recent paper titles or abstracts each. Pick the **2–3 whose topics overlap your brand** (supply chain, operations, risk/governance, AI adoption).
3. Favor someone who **teaches a class you're in or will take** — the relationship already has a foothold.

**Lead time is long** (faculty reply slowly; projects ramp over weeks), so per [MASTER-SCHEDULE](MASTER-SCHEDULE.md):
- [ ] **Week 2 (Jul 20–26):** *draft* the outreach email (template below).
- [ ] **Week 3 (Jul 27–Aug 2):** *send* it to your top 1–2 professors.

**Copy-paste outreach email:**

```
Subject: MSCM student interested in your [supply chain / risk / ops] research

Hi Professor [Last Name],

I'm Bhumika Choudhary, a current MSCM student at Foster. Before the program
I spent [X] years at Uber as a Global SME in Risk & Compliance, where I
worked on [fraud triage / SOP design / stakeholder-facing risk programs].

I came across your work on [specific paper / topic], and it connects
directly to what I want to build my career around: bringing risk and
governance discipline into operations — including how teams adopt AI safely.

I'd love to help with a current or upcoming project — literature review,
data cleaning, analysis, or writing support. I'm comfortable with
[Excel / SQL / basic Python] and can commit roughly [3–4] hours a week
this quarter.

Could I grab 15 minutes during your office hours to hear what you're
working on and where I might be useful?

Thank you for your time,
Bhumika Choudhary
[LinkedIn URL] · [phone/email]
```

*Tip: send one email per professor, personalized to their actual paper. Never BCC a batch.*

## 4. Optional Certifications
*Only if a specific gap or job posting demands it — otherwise your portfolio is the stronger signal.*

| Cert | When to say yes | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Lean Six Sigma Green Belt** (upgrade from Yellow) | You keep seeing "Green Belt preferred" in target JDs, or you want a credential that pairs with your DMAIC project (#17). Natural next step from what you have. | Medium — self-paced course + exam |
| **Google Project Management (Coursera)** | You want a recognizable PM credential fast and a structured refresher on PM fundamentals. Good résumé keyword; light lift. | Low–medium — ~1–2 months part-time |
| **PMP exam** | **Deferred — per your call.** You're already PMP-*trained*; sitting the exam is a strong signal but a big time sink. Revisit *after* December if a target role explicitly requires the full credential. | High — not this cycle |

**Reality check:** your data showed PMP appears in only ~19% of PM JDs and certs rarely beat a live portfolio. Treat certs as tie-breakers, not priorities.

## When to Say YES to an Edge Opportunity

Run any edge move through this filter. **If it fails even one, decline — protect the core plan.**

- [ ] **Timing:** it does *not* collide with a flagship deadline or exam week ([MASTER-SCHEDULE](MASTER-SCHEDULE.md)).
- [ ] **Leverage:** it produces a reusable asset — a résumé line, a [portfolio](02-projects.md) piece, or a real relationship — not just a busy weekend.
- [ ] **On-brand:** it strengthens the story *"strategic problem-solver who brings risk/governance discipline into ops & AI."*
- [ ] **Energy:** you actually have the bandwidth this week (not stealing from sleep, gym, or family time 🔒).

> **Rule of thumb:** at most **one** active edge move at a time. Core plan first, always. A missed edge opportunity costs nothing; a missed flagship costs you December.

**Pre-scheduled edge check-ins** (from [MASTER-SCHEDULE](MASTER-SCHEDULE.md)): Week 2 (research + draft prof email) · Week 3 (send prof email) · Week 8 (register a hackathon/comp if one lands Sep–Oct) · Weeks 11 & 13 (hackathon prep/weekend) · Week 16 (case comp if live).
