# Networking Engine

[← Index](INDEX.md)

> **Why this page exists:** networking is the single highest-ROI activity in your whole plan. Most jobs are filled through a warm intro, not a portal. Your portfolio proves you *can* do the work — but a person deciding to vouch for you is what gets you in the room. This page is built to be **grabbed and used**: open it, copy a message, fill the `[brackets]`, hit send.

**Your one-line intro (use everywhere):** *"Process & operations leader who makes things run better and cheaper — I bring risk, compliance & governance discipline into operations."*

Related pages: [Skills](01-skills.md) · [Projects](02-projects.md) · [Reading](04-reading-list.md) · [Consulting](05-consulting.md) · [Edge](06-edge.md)

## 1. The weekly cadence (non-negotiable blocks)

| Slot | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Chat #1** | Thu (during your 3–4 hr free block) | 1 coffee chat / alumni call |
| **Chat #2** | Fri | 2nd alumni chat *or* a warm-lead call |
| **Event** | Fri | 1 networking event (Foster mixer, industry meetup, webinar, panel) |
| **1 DM** | Mon (before class) | Send 1 new outreach message to keep the pipeline full |
| **Review** | Sun (15 min) | Update the tracker below; queue next week's asks |

**The math:** 2 chats/week × ~20 build weeks = **40+ real conversations before you ever apply.** That is why December is *not* a cold start — by then people already know you and are watching for openings.

**The rule that makes it work:** applications stay light until December (your portfolio isn't ready yet — see [Projects](02-projects.md)) **but networking runs hot the entire time.** Relationships take weeks to warm; skills you can cram. So front-load the slow thing.

**Mindset:** you're not asking for a job. You're asking to *learn* — about a team, a company, a career path. Curiosity is warm and gets replies. Desperation is cold and gets ignored. Every template below is built on that.

## 2. Copy-paste message templates

> Fill every `[bracket]`. Keep them short — long messages read as work. Send LinkedIn connection notes and short DMs; save the longer versions for email or a message *after* they connect.

### Template 1 — Cold alumni outreach (UW Foster)

```
Hi [Name], I'm Bhumika — an incoming UW Foster MSCM student (starting June 2026).
I came from a risk & compliance / operations background at Uber, and I'm exploring
[program management / operations] roles at companies like [Company].

I saw you went through Foster and are now at [Company] — I'd love to hear how that
transition went for you. Would you be open to a quick 15-min chat in the next couple
of weeks? Totally understand if you're heads-down.

Thanks either way — go Huskies!
Bhumika
```

### Template 2 — Warm follow-up / no-reply nudge

*Send ~5–7 days after no reply. One nudge only — then let it rest.*

```
Hi [Name], floating this back up in case it slipped past — totally get how busy things
get. Still would love 15 minutes to hear about your path to [Company / role] whenever
it's convenient. Happy to work entirely around your calendar. Thanks so much!
```

### Template 3 — Coffee-chat request (once they've replied / you have a warm lead)

```
Thank you, [Name]! Here are a few windows that work on my end — happy to flex to
whatever's easiest for you:
  • [Day, time]
  • [Day, time]
  • [Day, time]

15–20 minutes is plenty. I can call, Zoom, or grab coffee if you're in Seattle.
Really looking forward to it.
```

### Template 4 — Post-chat thank-you

*Send within 24 hours. Reference one specific thing — it proves you listened.*

```
Hi [Name], thank you so much for the time today — I really valued your perspective on
[specific thing they said, e.g. "how your team scopes a program before kickoff"].

The point about [second specific takeaway] especially stuck with me — I'm going to
[small concrete action you'll take because of it].

I'll keep you posted as I move through my search. Thanks again for being so generous
with your time!
Bhumika
```

### Template 5 — Referral ask

*Only after a real conversation — never on first contact. This is the Doc3 phrasing.*

```
Hi [Name], I really valued our conversation about [Company / team] — it confirmed this
is exactly the kind of work I want to move toward.

I noticed [Company] has an opening for [Role title] ([link]). Given what we talked
about, I think it's a strong fit for my ops + risk background. Would you be comfortable
referring me, or pointing me to the right person on the team? Totally understand if
it's not the right time — no pressure at all.

Either way, thank you — our chat genuinely helped me think about this more clearly.
```

### Template 6 — Networking-event follow-up

*Send within 48 hours of the event, while they still remember your face.*

```
Hi [Name], great to meet you at [event] — I really enjoyed our conversation about
[topic you discussed]. I'd love to stay connected as I dig into [program management /
operations] roles here in Seattle. Sending a connection request now — hope our paths
cross again soon!
```

### Template 7 — "Keep me on your radar" note

*For strong contacts you can't help right now — keeps the door open for December.*

```
Hi [Name], hope you're doing well! Quick update: I've been building out a portfolio of
operations / process-improvement case studies ([portfolio link]) and I'll be actively
interviewing starting this December.

No ask today — just wanted to stay on your radar. If you ever hear of a
[program / project management] opening on your team or nearby, I'd love for you to think
of me. Thanks, [Name] — always great staying in touch!
```

## 4. Coffee-chat question bank

> Ask 4–6, not all 10. Let it breathe — the best chats go off-script. Save the referral ask for *later* (Template 5), never this call.

1. How did you get into [role/team], and what surprised you most once you were in it?
2. What does a typical week actually look like — where does your time really go?
3. What separates someone who's *good* at this role from someone who's great?
4. What skills or tools does your team lean on most day-to-day? *(listen for Excel/SQL/risk — maps to [Skills](01-skills.md))*
5. When your team hires, what makes a candidate stand out — beyond the résumé?
6. How is [Company] thinking about [AI adoption / process automation] right now? *(your risk→AI thesis — a great differentiator)*
7. Looking back, what would you tell yourself starting a program/ops career today?
8. What's a problem your team is wrestling with that a fresh set of eyes could help on?
9. Is there anyone else you'd suggest I talk to as I learn about this space?
10. What's the best way to stay in touch — and how can I be useful to *you*?

**Pro moves:** #4 tells you what to build. #8 can become a portfolio project. #9 doubles your network from one chat. #10 flips the script — offering value is what makes you memorable.

## 5. Where to find people (and the small tactics)

**UW Foster alumni** — your warmest cold audience:
- **LinkedIn Alumni tool** → go to the *University of Washington (Foster School of Business)* page → **Alumni** tab → filter by *Where they work* and *What they do*. Gold for Template 1.
- **Foster Career Services / MSCM program staff** — ask for the alumni directory and any mentor-matching program. Use it.
- **Foster clubs & Slack/Discord** — join the ops/SCM and consulting clubs; current students refer you to recent grads.

**LinkedIn "Open to Work":**
- Turn it on **for recruiters only** (the private green-ring-off setting), *not* the public banner. Recruiters can find you; hiring managers and your network don't see a "job-seeking" flag. Keeps you looking selective, not searching.

**Handshake:**
- UW's official recruiting platform — employers who post there *want* students specifically. Set up your profile, follow your target companies, and watch for their on-campus events and info sessions (easy Friday event slots for §1).

**Events to fill the Friday slot:**
- Foster mixers & info sessions · Seattle ops/supply-chain meetups (Meetup.com) · industry webinars & panels · company info sessions on Handshake · [Edge](06-edge.md) case comps & hackathons (network *and* résumé line).

**Two habits that compound:**
- **Give before you ask.** Share an article, congratulate a promotion, intro two people. A network you've fed says yes in December.
- **Log every contact the same day** in the §3 tracker. A relationship you don't track is a relationship you'll lose.
